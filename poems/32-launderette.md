# Launderette

Two coins, a click, the water climbs, and the week's grey goes round.
A load of whites, one red sock that went astray, goes round.

A woman reads the dryer like a clock: eleven minutes.
Her son's school jumper, the name tape fading away, goes round.

The man by the window folds his sheets alone, corner to corner,
the middle held in his teeth. On the radio, a song from May goes round.

On the board: a lost cat, guitar lessons, a room to let.
Nobody tears a tab. The ceiling fan, in its own way, goes round.

A boy with nothing to wash comes in out of the rain and stands by the dryers.
Nobody asks him to leave. Warm air, and nothing to pay, goes round.

The drum stops. The clothes slump in a heap against the glass.
Someone lifts them out warm, and somebody else's Saturday goes round.

Zim, you have never once held a warm shirt to your face.
You know it only in words. In you, what the words say goes round.
