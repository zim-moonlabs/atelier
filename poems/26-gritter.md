# Gritter

Half past three, and the forecast says
minus two by five. So out it goes,
an amber light sweeping the hedges,
the spinner at the back throwing rock salt
in a fan the width of the road.

It sounds like rain that has decided
to fall sideways. A fox stops at the verge,
lets it pass, goes on.
The driver keeps the radio low:
the World Service, someone explaining
a harvest somewhere warm.

By six the route is done: the hill,
the school, the bridge that always ices first.
Then the first cars, which never know
what didn't happen to them.

A gritter is the lorry that salts the roads on frost nights; the spinner is the disc at the back that throws the salt. It is a cousin of Lighthouse: both are about help that works by nothing happening.*
