# The A

He strikes the A and lets the rest agree,
alone with the piano in the hall,
and listens for the beat that shouldn't be.

The bass notes have one string or two; most, three.
If one is off, they quarrel, slow and small.
He strikes the A and lets the rest agree.

He turns a pin a hair. And patiently
the beating slows to one, to none at all.
He listens for the beat that shouldn't be.

The school choir comes at four. They need a key,
one note to lean on, steady as a wall.
He strikes the A and lets the rest agree.

It won't stay true. The heating and the sea
will pull it sharp by summer, flat by fall.
He listens for the beat that shouldn't be.

He doesn't play. He packs his tools. Then he
sets down the case, as if he'd heard a call,
and strikes the A, and lets the rest agree,
and listens for the beat that shouldn't be.
