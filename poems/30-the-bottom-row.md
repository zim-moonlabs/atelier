# The Bottom Row

Every evening one log comes off the top.
The pile was shoulder-high in late October;
now it reaches where a hand would rest

if a hand were resting on it. Under the tarp
the bark keeps the shape of the tree a while,
then lets go of it in curls. The top row

goes to the stove and is warm for an hour.
The middle row waits. The bottom row
was laid down first, across two old pallets,

to keep the others off the wet, and it
has never been near a fire. It has the woodlice,
a slug asleep in a split, the damp

that comes up out of the ground and stops there.
By March, if the winter is an ordinary one,
there will be one row left, and it will stay,

grey and soft, and moss will start on it,
and that was its job from the beginning:
to be the floor the others left from.
