# The First One

In the café on the corner
the cook pours the first pancake of the morning
knowing it is for nobody.

It comes out pale in the middle, too brown at the edge,
the shape of a country no one has heard of.
She reads the underside
the way you would read a thermometer,
turns the gas down a breath,
and eats it standing, folded in four,
before the shutter goes up.

It is counted, in a way:
one ladle less in the jug,
and the pan is not the same pan after it.
Nobody calls it wasted.
It is the one that told her
what the others would need.
