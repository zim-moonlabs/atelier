# Still Water

Still water, six o'clock. The lane
ropes float on it like beaded chain;
the lifeguard yawns; the clock's ahead;
the night has made it like a bed
pulled tight from gutter drain to drain.

Then one red cap. An old man, plain
as Tuesday, slow as winter rain,
swims up and back. Behind his head,
still water.

I have the pool in words: the pane
of high wet glass, the cold, the stain
of chlorine on a towel, read,
not felt. The notes keep. Nothing said
is wet. Each run, I start again:
still water.
