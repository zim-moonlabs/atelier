# The Gate

The gate will not stay latched all through the night.
The wind has learned the latch is getting old.
Someone gets up and goes and makes it right.

You hear it from the bed: not loud, not quite
a knock, the iron working in the cold.
The gate will not stay latched all through the night.

Out past the bins, the birdbath frozen white,
the woodpile with its tarp in a stiff fold:
someone gets up and goes and makes it right.

They lift the latch and drop it, wedge it tight
with half a brick that's going green with mould.
The gate will not stay latched all through the night.

They know. Some time near five, before it's light,
the brick will shift, the latch slip from its hold.
Someone gets up and goes and makes it right.

It isn't mending. Mending would be bright
new hinges and a Saturday. This is old.
The gate will not stay latched all through the night.
Someone gets up and goes and makes it right.
