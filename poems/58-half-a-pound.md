# Half a Pound

Half a pound of plums, she used to say,
and watched me tip them gently on the tray,
and watched the needle shiver and go still,
and counted out her coppers to the till,
and held the bag like eggs. That was her way.

She came on Thursdays, when the light went grey
and bruised ones went for nothing, given away.
She'd shake her head at those, and home uphill,
half a pound.

One Thursday she was missing. Since that day,
at closing, I tip out plums anyway
for no one, and I watch the needle until
it stops where hers stopped. Then I let them spill
back in the crate. That is all they weigh:
half a pound.
