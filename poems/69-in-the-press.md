# In the Press

The last thing she does before she locks up is case in the book: paste on the endpapers, close the covers over the text block, square it once on the bench with the flat of her hand. Then it goes between two pressing boards, brass-edged, the raised edge laid in the groove of the joint so it sets sharp, and into the standing press, and she turns the iron bar until it will not turn without her shoulder.

The paste is wet. Wet paper grows. If she took the book out now it would dry in the room's own time, one side faster than the other, and by morning the boards would be bowed like a hand half open. In the press it can only dry flat. That is all the press does. It does not glue anything. It only refuses, all night, to let the book move.

She has a customer who asks every time why it takes until tomorrow. The sewing is done, the cover is made, he can see it is finished. She tells him: it is finished, it is not yet a book.

> the bar turned and left
> nothing in the dark workshop
> moves but the drying

In the morning she unscrews it, slowly, the way you would take your hand off something you have been holding still. She opens the book once at the middle and listens for the spine. Then she closes it and it stays closed by itself, which is the whole test.
