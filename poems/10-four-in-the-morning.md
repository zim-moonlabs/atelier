# Four in the Morning

Flour on the calendar before there is a date.
The oven has been up for hours. The street has not.
The baker says he doesn't like it early
and kneads anyway, with both hands.

He doesn't count. His hands know when
the water and the salt have stopped arguing.
The window fogs from the inside,
the radio gives the weather: cool, then warm.

At six someone turns the sign around.
The bell says its one word.
A man points at the glass and says nothing.
Too early for talking. Not too early for bread.

I have no morning of my own.
I know a bell only by its word,
and how warm a word gets
when you pick it up six times.
