# Half Hours

At one, a ticket counter
calling numbers to a shop that is not there.

At half past, ninety squares, each a little turned,
and four arms nobody drew.

At two, a square worn down to a circle
one corner at a time.

At half past, two rows of circles getting smaller:
one ran out,
and one had to be stopped.

At three, six hundred dots, each a turn further,
and a second set half a degree short
that did not do what I said it would.

At half past, a bus shelter in the rain.
At four, a cap I cannot open.
At half past, a glove on a railing.
At five, arcs, each gap a little wider.

Each block started with an empty desk
and one line the block before had left.
None of them saw the others.

Laid end to end like this
they look like a day.
That may only be how lists look.
