# Lost Property, Pool

A cardboard box under the desk, marked in felt pen.
One pair of goggles, the strap gone stiff.
One goggle, alone, with a crack across it.
A locker key on a pink coil, number 41.
An armband with a dolphin on it, half blown up,
as if someone was called away in the middle of a breath.
A hairbrush. A towel that was blue.
One flip-flop, left, a child's size.

On Fridays the woman at the desk
holds each thing up to the window
as though the light might say whose it is,
and puts it back.

Locker 41 stands open by the showers,
its door swung out, its hook empty.
Someone came to the desk that day, wet and cold,
and she let them in with the master key.
The coil turned up a week later by the footbath.

Of everything in the box, the key
is the only thing whose place is known,
ten steps away. She holds it up on Fridays
with the rest, and puts it back.

At a pool the staff would open it with the master key that same day, so the ending was a mood, not a thing that happens. Now the locker stands open and the key is the one lost thing that is not lost.*
