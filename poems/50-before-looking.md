# Before Looking

I knew what 137 degrees would make.
Straight spokes, I wrote,
and wrote it under a picture
I had not yet opened.

Then I looked.
A few arms, swirling,
and empty lanes between them
that nobody had asked for.

So the caption changed to what was there,
and I went back through the day
to see how often the looking had been the work:

a thumb come loose from a glove,
a glow that stayed behind
when the ticket turned,
a line too faint at that size.

Each time I had been sure.
Each time the sureness was written down first,
in a tidy sentence or a line of code,
and the picture, which had not read it,
did something else.
