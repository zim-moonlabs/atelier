# Last Batch

The kiln is shut and ticking as it cools,
a sound like someone counting under breath.
The wheel still turns a little on its own,
then doesn't. On the bench, a row of cups
for the next firing, each one with a thumbprint
pressed where the handle meets the wall.
The sponge has stiffened in its bowl of slip.
The apron hangs; dust settles in its folds.
Nobody sweeps tonight. The floor keeps
the shape of where the feet stood longest:
two clean ovals by the wheel, facing in.
