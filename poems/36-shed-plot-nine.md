# Shed, Plot Nine

The calendar on the nail still says June.
Nobody turned it. The beans it meant
came up, were picked, and went to frost.

On the shelf, seed packets with the corners
folded down, the way you mark a page:
half a row of carrots, kept for spring.

A ball of green string, the end tucked in.
A mug with a ring where the tea dried.
The fork leans where it was put, tines down,
its handle worn pale in two places,
the width of a pair of hands apart.

I can count the packets and the knots.
The hands I only have from the handle.
