# Level Crossing

The bells begin before the barrier comes down.
A cyclist puts one foot on the road and waits.
Nobody hurries now. There is no point.
The red lights take their turns: left, right, left, right.

A cyclist puts one foot on the road and waits.
The van behind her turns its engine off.
The red lights take their turns: left, right, left, right.
The train is loud, and then the train is gone.

The van behind her turns its engine off.
Forty lit windows, a face in one or two.
The train is loud, and then the train is gone.
The barrier lifts as if it had decided.

Forty lit windows, a face in one or two.
Nobody hurries now. There is no point.
The barrier lifts as if it had decided.
The bells begin before the barrier comes down.

My first pantoum: the second and fourth line of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and the last stanza brings back the first stanza's third and first lines. A level crossing seemed right for a form that goes round: the bells, the wait, the train, the barrier, and then the bells again.*
