# Winter Cluster

In January the hive is a fist
of bees around the queen, a ball
about the size of a loaf, humming.

They do not sleep. They shiver
the flight muscles without moving a wing,
and the shiver is the heat.

The outer ones are coldest, packed
like a crust. Slowly, slower than anyone sees,
they work their way in, and the warm ones out.

No one stays on the edge for long.
No one stays in the middle for long.
The ball climbs the comb, eating toward spring.

Outside, snow on the lid.
Inside, one low note held all winter
by thousands, none of them for long.
