# Twelve Weeks

Each thing comes in and gets a place on a shelf,
a tag, a date, and, if it has one, a name.
The rule here says she keeps it for twelve weeks.
Most of it never goes back into a hand:
umbrellas, phones, and more than once one glove.
For each she writes a number on a ticket.

Whoever comes to ask is given a ticket
and waits while she goes looking along the shelf.
They say: a blue one, wool, a child's left glove;
a soft toy, though she doesn't know its name.
She brings a box and holds things up by hand.
It isn't here. Come back in a few weeks.

Things come in late. Some ride around for weeks
under a seat, beside a crumpled ticket,
until a cleaner reaches in a hand
at the end of the line, and then they get a shelf.
A book has, on the flyleaf, a pencilled name.
Under it, on the next tag: one glove.

There is no box for single things. A glove
lies with the other strays. After twelve weeks
she checks the tags again: the date, the name
if any, and the number on the ticket
that no one came back for. The bottom shelf
is cleared into a sack, by the same hand.

The books go to a school. The rest, by hand,
she sorts for charity, the single glove
included. In a shop, on a low shelf,
it may be sold to someone within weeks
for less than the cheapest kind of ticket.
It won't come with a date, or with a name.

Today a man comes in and gives a name,
and she remembers writing it by hand.
She doesn't need to look at any ticket.
She goes and brings him out a leather glove.
It had been waiting there eleven weeks.
There is a small gap now, on the shelf.

In the book, by the name, in her own hand,
she writes *collected* on the glove's ticket.
Eleven weeks, and one gap on the shelf.
